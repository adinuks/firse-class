# Copy playbook: punchy, calm, specific

Punchy here does not mean loud. It means every line could not be shorter without losing something. Think of reading aloud: each line gets one breath.

## The shape of a line

- **Headline:** 2–6 words (six is a hard limit; count them), a complete thought, ends with a full stop. A claim or a promise, never a label.
- **Sub-line:** one sentence, ≤ 20 words, says how or why. It lifts the headline instead of repeating it.
- **Body:** two to three short sentences at most. If a paragraph runs longer, the section is doing two jobs; split it.
- **CTA:** a verb and nothing else ("See how it works", "Get started", "Read the story"). Secondary actions are plain text links with an arrow.

## Moves that work

1. **Fragments.** "Fast. Quiet. Yours." A fragment stands taller than a sentence.
2. **Rule of three**, then break it. Three short beats, with the last one carrying the payoff.
3. **Contrast pair.** "Complex inside. Simple outside." Two opposites, balanced.
4. **Specific over adjective.** Swap "powerful" for the number or the thing it lets you do. "All day." beats "long-lasting battery".
5. **Benefit first, spec second.** Lead with what the person gets. Put the mechanism in the smaller line beneath.
6. **Second person, present tense.** "You focus. It handles the rest."
7. **Name the thing once, plainly.** No taglines stacked on taglines.
8. **Let the pause work.** A one-word line on its own is allowed once or twice per page, not more.

## What to cut

- Exclamation marks, rhetorical questions in headlines, emoji.
- "Revolutionary", "world-class", "cutting-edge", "best-in-class", "seamless", "leverage", "synergy".
- "We are proud to…", "Welcome to…", "Our mission is…". Start with the person's gain or the fact itself.
- Hedging ("can help you", "designed to", "aims to"). Say what it does.
- Two ideas joined by "and" in a headline. Pick one.

## Before and after (generic examples to model the move, not to reuse)

| Before | After |
|---|---|
| Welcome to our innovative platform that helps teams collaborate more effectively | Work together. Without the noise. |
| Our course is designed to help you develop skills in design leadership | Learn to lead design. |
| Industry-leading performance with best-in-class reliability | Fast. And it stays fast. |
| We have over 20 years of experience in the industry | 20+ years. One craft. |

The last row only works if the number is a real fact the user gave you, with the same precision: "more than 20" is "20+", not "twenty". Never borrow a number to make a line land.

## Page rhythm

Hero headline → one-sentence sub-line → CTA. Then each section: headline (short) → a single supporting line or number → optional detail below the fold. End with a quiet closing line and the primary action again. Read the headlines in sequence; they should tell the whole story on their own.

## Self-check before building

- Can every headline be read in one breath?
- Is there any adjective that a number or a verb could replace?
- Does each section say one thing?
- Is every fact traceable to what the user supplied?
